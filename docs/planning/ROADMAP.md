# st0n3r — Roadmap to Real Literature

_Drafted at milestone 1 (v0.2.0, PR #1 merged). The question this roadmap
answers: what does st0n3r need to stop producing competent generated prose
and start producing literature — and to be a tool a serious writer reaches
for daily?_

The organizing belief: literature is not better sentences. It is **voice,
interiority, structure, truth, and revision** — five things current AI
writing tools don't even measure. Each feature below attacks one of them.

---

## 1. The Voice Engine

A stylometric fingerprint per project: sentence-shape distributions, clause
rhythm, diction register, punctuation habits, dialogue-tag economy — learned
from exemplar texts the writer feeds it (their own prose, or authors they'd
steal from if they could). Every draft gets a **voice-drift score** against
the fingerprint, chapter over chapter, the way slop is scored today; the
revise stage gets "this paragraph broke voice HERE" findings with the
fingerprint's evidence. Style stops being a paragraph of adjectives in
style.md and becomes something the harness can hold you to. *(Voice is the
first thing literature has and generated prose lacks.)*

## 2. Character Interiority Agents

Major characters become persistent agents with **private state the narrator
cannot see**: what they know (bounded by the timeline — a character cannot
act on chapter-9 information in chapter 4, and the harness can prove it),
what they want, what they're lying about, what they refuse to say. Dialogue
scenes are generated by putting the relevant character agents in a room and
letting them collide, then the writer-narrator renders the collision. Kills
the everyone-sounds-the-same disease and buys dramatic irony, subtext, and
characters who surprise the author — the actual mechanics of interiority.

## 3. Draft Tournaments

Milestone 1 learned from autonovel that absolute scores collapse but
comparisons don't. So: never draft once. Generate N takes of a scene from
deliberately different angles (start late / start early, close / distant,
scene / half-scene), run **blind pairwise Elo judging** across a panel,
advance the winner, then graft the losers' best moves onto it. Expose it in
the UI as a draft room where the writer can also vote — their votes train
the project's taste. Cost scales with how much a chapter matters; openings
and endings earn big tournaments.

## 4. Pacing Instrumentation (breaking the pacing ceiling)

Pacing was autonovel's admitted unsolved weakness, and no LLM judge fixes
it, because pacing is structural. Build the instruments: **tension curves**
(per-scene stakes/valence annotation), scene-vs-summary ratio maps,
beat-drift detection (planned beats vs delivered), chapter-length and
POV-cadence rhythm, structure overlays (three-act, Freytag, Story Grid) on
a manuscript timeline in the dashboard. Findings become review-pass inputs:
"chapters 9–12 flatline; nothing changes hands." Make pacing visible and
it becomes fixable.

## 5. The Writers' Room

Replace one-shot review passes with **persistent, named editors with
memory**: a developmental editor, a line editor, a continuity pedant, a
first reader — each keeps a running notebook across the whole project,
remembers what they flagged last round, notices whether you addressed it,
and escalates or lets go accordingly. They disagree with each other on the
record; the writer moderates. Margin comments become first-class: annotate
any span in the UI, and that annotation is context the room must answer to.
This is the difference between running a linter and having an editor.

## 6. The Verisimilitude Engine

Literature is dense with true detail; models hallucinate confidently. Add a
**research role with web access** that builds a project *fact locker*
(period detail, procedure, geography, law, jargon — with sources), feeds
concrete detail to the writer agent on demand, and runs an **anachronism /
error sweep** over the manuscript against the locker. For a book like
*Sungrown*: real permit forms, real cultivation practice, real county
process. The difference between "she filled out paperwork" and the name of
the form.

## 7. Promise & Motif Ledger

Threads track plot; literature also runs on **images and promises**. Track
every planted gun, motif, and image with its intended payoff; report unfired
guns before "done" is allowed. Map motif recurrence and spacing across the
manuscript (the heatmap makes symbol-laziness visible). Measure whether the
ending **rhymes** with the opening — lexical and imagistic echo between
first and last chapters — because endings that land almost always do.

## 8. Reader Simulation at Scale

A synthetic focus group: dozens of distinct reader personas (not four) read
chapter by chapter and log *live* reactions — bored-here, confused-here,
stopped-reading-here, read-that-line-twice — aggregated into an
**attention heatmap over the whole book**. Benchmark chapters in blind
pairwise reads against public-domain comps in the same register ("does this
hold up next to Williams? where exactly does it fold?"). Synthetic readers
are not readers, but a hundred of them disagreeing in patterns is signal no
single reviewer pass can give.

## 9. Draft Archaeology

Real books are rewritten, not written. Build the revision layer writers
actually need: every draft of every chapter preserved and diffable (git
underneath, writer-friendly on top), **sentence-level provenance** (which
round produced this line and why — the ledger already knows), restore any
paragraph from any draft, A/B reading views, and **structural refactors as
first-class operations**: merge these two chapters, move this reveal three
chapters earlier, change this POV — each executed with automatic archivist
+ continuity re-verification of everything downstream. autonovel merged
24 chapters into 19; that operation should be one command with receipts.

## 10. The Production Line

A book isn't done until it reaches readers. Typeset trade-paperback PDF and
EPUB (the whole manuscript, professionally set), Shunn-format submission
DOCX, synopsis and query-letter generation from canon (which already knows
the whole book), a cover design brief, and a **multi-voice table-read**
audiobook draft (character-attributed dialogue → distinct voices) — because
hearing dialogue exposes clunk that no eye catches. `stoner ship` takes the
manuscript and hands you the package.

---

## Beyond the ten (parking lot)

- **Series canon**: multi-book universes, character progressions across
  books, cross-book continuity checks.
- **Voice fine-tunes**: a small local model tuned on the writer's own prose
  drafting voice-true, big models revising.
- **Nonfiction mode**: citation-grade fact locker, argument-structure
  instrumentation instead of tension curves.
- **Collaboration**: two writers, one canon; roles and sign-offs.

## Sequencing sketch

Voice Engine (1) and Pacing Instrumentation (4) first — they're measurement,
and everything else improves once quality is measurable. Then Writers' Room
(5) + Draft Tournaments (3) as the new revision core; Interiority Agents (2)
and Verisimilitude (6) as the new drafting core; Promise Ledger (7) and
Reader Simulation (8) as the new judgment core; Archaeology (9) throughout;
Production Line (10) when books start finishing.
