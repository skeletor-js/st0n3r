When California legalization arrives with a permit process, a corporate buyout offer, and a market that no longer needs her, 61-year-old Humboldt grower Ruth Vann must decide whether forty years of clandestine, exacting labor can survive becoming legal — or whether legality is just prohibition's quieter way of taking the same thing.

## Themes

<!-- The 1-3 ideas the book is actually *about*, underneath the plot. Not
     morals or messages — questions the story keeps asking without
     answering flatly. -->

- the dignity and erasure of skilled labor under bureaucratic and market systems
- what a life's meaning is worth once the thing that gave it danger, and therefore value, is gone
- land and craft as inheritance versus land and craft as commodity

## Promise to the Reader

<!-- What experience are you selling? What kind of ending does this genre's
     reader expect? State it here so you don't quietly swap genres on them
     at the midpoint. What must *not* happen for the promise to hold? -->

This is a novel of quiet accumulation, not plot: the pleasure is watching an unglamorous, competent woman's entire adult life get weighed, itemized, and found either compliant or not by systems that were never built to see her. For the promise to hold, there can be no heist, no raid, no courtroom win, no romance that redeems her — only the slow, exact record of what she loses and what, if anything, she keeps that no permit can revoke. The ending should land the way Stoner's does: not defeat, not triumph, but a plain, earned reckoning with a life fully seen on its own terms.
