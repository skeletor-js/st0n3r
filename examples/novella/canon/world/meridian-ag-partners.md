## Rules

<!-- Hard constraints: what this place/system/faction can and cannot do.
     For magic or tech systems, this is your consistency contract — write
     it precisely enough that a violation would visibly break a rule
     written here, not just "feel off." -->

Meridian buys only fully permitted, fully compliant parcels — it will not touch a grow still mid-process, will not purchase plants, genetics, or equipment, and will not assume any pending code violation. Its standard offer is cash for land plus permit, structured so the seller receives a lump sum but forfeits any right to cultivate under that permit again, anywhere in the county, for five years. It employs no growers directly on legacy hillside parcels; once acquired, small parcels are typically left fallow or absorbed into a larger consolidated grow elsewhere with flat, irrigated, mechanized ground. It cannot legally lowball beyond appraised land value, but appraisal excludes the value of an existing customer base, reputation, or genetics — only acreage, water rights, and structures are counted. Its representatives are required by their own internal policy to make one offer, in writing, and may not return with a second offer for twelve months.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

Meridian formed in 2019 from a merger of two Central Valley agriculture investment funds looking to move into cannabis once federal banking restrictions loosened enough to make it viable at scale. It has spent three years quietly acquiring compliant permits across Humboldt and Trinity counties, consolidating small canopy allotments into a handful of large, mechanized grows near Willow Creek. Ruth Vann's parcel — undersized, on a seasonal road, non-compliant on the water question — is not a parcel Meridian wants for cultivation. It wants the permit's canopy allotment, bankable and transferable, to add to a project elsewhere. She is, to them, a line item.
