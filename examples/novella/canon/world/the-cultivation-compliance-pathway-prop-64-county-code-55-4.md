## Rules

<!-- Hard constraints: what this place/system/faction can and cannot do.
     For magic or tech systems, this is your consistency contract — write
     it precisely enough that a violation would visibly break a rule
     written here, not just "feel off." -->

A legal cultivation license requires: (1) a CEQA environmental review of the parcel, paid for by the applicant, with no cap on cost or timeline; (2) all-weather road access rated for emergency vehicles, meaning any grower on a seasonal or unpaved road must grade and rock it before permitting, at an average cost exceeding what a small grow earns in two seasons; (3) a water right or approved storage-and-forbearance permit — diversion during the dry season (April-October) is prohibited regardless of how long a well or spring has been used; (4) a METRC track-and-trace tag affixed to every individual plant from clone to sale, scanned at each stage, with untagged plants counted as black-market regardless of the grower's history; (5) a fixed canopy limit tied to zoning, which cannot be exceeded even if the land could support more. Violation of any single provision voids the license outright; there is no partial compliance. The system does not distinguish forty years of clean cultivation from zero — every applicant starts at the same blank form. Permits are non-transferable except through county-approved sale, which requires the same review process to restart under the new owner.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

Passed in the wake of Proposition 64, the county code was written by people who had never grown a plant on a hillside with no grid power, modeled instead on wine-country vineyard permitting. It assumes flat land, paved access, and metered water — none of which describes the terrain most legacy growers, including Ruth Vann, actually farm. The first permit cycle in 2017 saw the vast majority of applications in Ruth's watershed rejected or withdrawn before completion; by the time of the novel's present, entire ridgelines of once-cultivated land sit fallow, their owners either bought out or simply stopped.
