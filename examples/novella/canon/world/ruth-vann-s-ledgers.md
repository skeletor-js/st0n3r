## Rules

<!-- Hard constraints: what this place/system/faction can and cannot do.
     For magic or tech systems, this is your consistency contract — write

Nineteen spiral-bound and marbled composition notebooks, one per year from 1985 to the present, each recording strain, mother-plant lineage, transplant and flower dates, cure duration, trim hours by hand versus machine, and the name of every buyer paid in cash. The ledgers contain no financial totals that would satisfy an accountant or a court — no receipts, no tax figures, no addresses — because they were never built to be legible to anyone outside the trade; they record craft, not commerce. They have no legal standing: they cannot be submitted as proof of prior cultivation for permit priority, cannot establish water use history, and cannot be shown to a buyer's spreadsheet as evidence of quality, because the buyer's spreadsheet has no column for cure duration or trim technique. They can only be read by someone who already knows what they mean.

## Description

<!-- Sensory, concrete detail. What it looks, sounds, and smells like. -->

Notebooks gone soft and swollen at the spine from being handled with damp hands after washing trim scissors, ink faded to brown in the earliest volumes, a rubber band snapped and re-snapped around the stack. Ruth's handwriting tightens and steadies across the decades — looping and uncertain in 1985, cramped and fast by the 2000s, then large again in recent years, as if her hand has slowed.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

Started the first season Ruth cultivated on her own, when a buyer stiffed her and she began writing everything down out of spite rather than sentiment. Over four decades the habit outlived its original purpose and became something closer to a private history of the mountain — genetics she selected and renamed, seasons that failed, the handful of growers who taught her and are now dead or gone. When the county's permit application asks for 'documentation establishing prior commercial cultivation,' the ledgers are the only record Ruth has, and the only one the county will not accept.
