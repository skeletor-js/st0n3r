## Rules

<!-- Hard constraints: what this place/system/faction can and cannot do.
     For magic or tech systems, this is your consistency contract — write
     it precisely enough that a violation would visibly break a rule
     written here, not just "feel off." -->

Administered by the Humboldt County Planning and Building Department; hearings and appeals are heard in Eureka, the county seat. Structure setbacks are fixed at 50 ft from any parcel line for cultivation or accessory structures and 100 ft from the top of bank of any Class I or Class II watercourse — Ruth's generator shed violates both. Water certification follows the State Water Board's Cannabis Policy: any diversion off a Class I or II stream, permitted or not, must cease during the forbearance period, June 1 through October 31; Ruth's 1987 diversion has no water right of record — no pre-1914 claim, no riparian filing — draws exactly during that forbidden window, and sits on a stream reach where the only certificated right is now held downstream by Canopy Ag Holdings, which forecloses any path to after-the-fact registration. The sole compliance route is off-season storage: 30,000+ gallons of tankage filled November through March, installed cost floor roughly $62,000. Every cultivator over 8 inches of vegetative growth must carry an RFID tag in GrowTrack, the state-mandated track-and-trace platform; every harvest lot is logged by weight at wet, dry, and trimmed stages, and nothing can be sold without an unbroken tag chain — any discrepancy over 5% between tagged and logged weight triggers a mandatory audit. Cell and data service at Ruth's elevation is unreliable, so tagging often has to be done in batches when she can get a signal. Violations are sorted into three categories: Category I (paperwork, 30-day cure, no fee), Category II (setback, water source, or structural — 60-day cure, $1,200 reinspection fee, and an engineered remediation plan), and Category III (repeat findings or any watershed discharge — cultivation suspended pending a public hearing, permit revocable). A grower who can't cure everything in time may be offered a Conditional Reduced-Scale License: total canopy capped at 5,000 sq ft regardless of existing footprint, cultivation consolidated into one contiguous compliant zone, sales restricted exclusively to licensed distributor pickup — no direct sale to processors or dispensaries — and no full-sun cultivation permitted on any parcel carrying an uncured Category II finding.

## Description

<!-- Sensory, concrete detail. What it looks, sounds, and smells like. -->

The hearing room is on an upper floor of the Planning and Building Department building on H Street, fluorescent-lit, folding chairs bolted to nothing, a county seal behind the dais going slightly yellow at the edges. One wall holds a laminated parcel map color-coded by permit status — green for active, yellow for corrective action, red for suspended — updated by hand with a grease pencil when the printer's behind. Planners read violation codes off a checklist in a flat, unhurried voice, the same tone whether the fine is $1,200 or the finding could end someone's operation.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

The ordinance passed in 2016 following the state's legalization measure and was amended in 2019, after public outcry over the size of incoming corporate applications, to cap canopy on all new permits. Canopy Ag Holdings filed its core acreage before that amendment took effect and was grandfathered in at the old, unlimited scale — meaning the door closed on canopy size for everyone who applied after, Ruth among them, the same year it stayed open for the operation now next door.
