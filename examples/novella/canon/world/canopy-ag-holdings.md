A corporate cannabis cultivator and processor that has assembled 214 contiguous acres in the Salmon Creek drainage: its original 38-acre purchase in 2018, the 60-acre Ostrander parcel bought from Denny Ostrander in 2019, the 74-acre Ferro parcel, and the 42-acre Higgins parcel bought in 2021. Its core permits were filed under the pre-2019 unlimited-canopy provision and grandfathered when the ordinance changed, giving it roughly 40 acres of active mixed-light canopy — no comparable cap to what legacy or new applicants face. Canopy holds a standing acquisition offer for adjoining legacy parcels: $9,500/acre for raw acreage, $14,000/acre if the parcel carries an active or approved permit application, in which case Canopy also assumes remaining corrective-action costs and completes them with its own compliance staff. As part of the 2019 Ostrander sale, Wade Ostrander — Denny's brother — sold his senior 1958 riparian water right on the shared tributary to Canopy, which now holds the only certificated water right on that stream reach, the fact that forecloses certification of Ruth's diversion. Wholesale price collapse is fixed and consistent across the timeline: pre-2016 top-shelf sun-grown ran $2,200–$2,600/lb; by 2026, compliant licensed flower with a clean GrowTrack chain averages $260–$420/lb wholesale, while flower without a clean tag chain is confined to a shrinking cash market paying $150–$200/lb. A local contractor, Loop Construction, quotes $46,000 flat for the engineered retaining wall, regrading, and culvert relocation required to bring a generator shed and terrace edge into setback compliance — the figure Ruth weighs against Canopy's buyout offer for her parcel.

## Description

<!-- Sensory, concrete detail. What it looks, sounds, and smells like. -->

The greenhouse complex is visible from the ridge road a mile off, white poly and glass catching the light, semi-trucks with the company's green leaf logo idling at the loading dock, chain link and cameras at a gate that used to be a cattle guard. In the IGA parking lot in Redway, Canopy's white utility trucks now sit alongside the older pickups with plywood-patched beds that belong to what's left of the small growers — the Petrakis place, Sissy Yarnell's co-op — who talk about the buyouts in town only in the past tense, and only about other people's.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

Canopy entered the valley in 2018 with the Ostrander purchase and expanded through 2021, buying out growers who couldn't survive the price collapse or the permit process on their own. Some who sold say plainly they're relieved not to be carrying two mortgages on land that stopped paying for itself; others who sold don't talk about it in town at all. Ruth's is one of the last non-contiguous parcels bordering the Canopy footprint that hasn't sold.
