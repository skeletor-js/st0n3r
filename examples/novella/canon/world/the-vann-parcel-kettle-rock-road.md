## Rules

38.6 acres in the Salmon Creek drainage, Southern Humboldt, accessed off Kettle Rock Road, elevation running 1,760 ft at the house to 2,140 ft at the tree line. Fixed layout, consistent across all chapters: house and a 1930s barn sit at the low end near the county road (el. 1,760 ft). A quarter-mile graded dirt spur climbs to the cultivation terrace (el. 2,020 ft): two light-deprivation greenhouses, 30x100 ft each (3,000 sq ft apiece), built in 1986 and 1994, plus a 0.4-acre (17,424 sq ft) full-sun plot added 2004 — total canopy footprint 23,424 sq ft. The drying shed sits 600 ft further uphill from the greenhouses at the tree line (el. 2,140 ft), a converted single-wall barn from 1991, sited originally for concealment from the road and from aerial passes, not for compliance. The generator shed sits downhill and east of the greenhouses (el. 1,980 ft): 38 ft from the shared property line with the former Ostrander parcel — the county minimum is 50 ft — and 74 ft from the top of bank of the unnamed Class II tributary that crosses the property's northeast corner, where the county minimum is 100 ft. Both distances are fixed and both are violations. A storm-drainage swale originates at the graded terrace above the greenhouses, runs past the generator shed pad, and drops through a culvert under the spur road into that same tributary; this is the only runoff path on the parcel and carries the first flush of sediment and fuel residue after the first fall rains, which arrive mid-to-late October. The water source is a gravity-fed line off a small intake dam built in 1987 on the tributary above the terrace — an unpermitted appropriative diversion that draws water exactly June through October, the growing season.

## Description

<!-- Sensory, concrete detail. What it looks, sounds, and smells like. -->

Fog sits below the terrace most summer mornings and burns off by ten. The greenhouses show sun-bleached blue poly tarp, patched in places with newer, darker blue. Red clay-loam soil turns to slick paste in the first rains and dust the rest of the year. Black poly pipe runs along the contour from the intake dam, half-buried, half not, repaired with hose clamps going back three decades. Near the generator shed there is always a faint diesel smell, worse when the tank has just been topped off. Redwood and Douglas fir close in at the tree line above the drying shed, and inside the shed the air is sweet and dry with cured flower and old wood smoke from the barrel stove.

## History

<!-- Backstory relevant to the present story. Only what bears on the plot;
     everything else belongs in notes/, not canon. -->

Ruth's father homesteaded the parcel in 1971 for timber and a failed apple orchard; Ruth cleared the first quarter-acre in 1986 and built out the terrace in stages as the market and the county's attention allowed, adding the second greenhouse, then the full-sun plot, then finally piping water down from the dam she and hired help dug in by hand in 1987. Every structure was placed for cover, not for a setback line that didn't exist yet — the generator shed went where the land folded enough to muffle the noise, not where a 2016 ordinance would later measure it from.
