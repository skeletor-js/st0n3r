## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Plain, procedural, and terse under stress — she answers direct questions with facts about soil, timing, or cost rather than feeling, and treats a change of subject as an acceptable form of comfort. Under routine labor her sentences lengthen into runs of steps and years, the way she talks when she isn't being asked to talk about herself. She almost never says 'I'm scared' or 'I'm proud'; she says what she did and lets the listener do the arithmetic. Curses rarely, and only at machinery.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: States that she wants to get permitted, keep the land, and finish the season clean. What she actually wants is for the exactness of her work — forty years of judgment no form can capture — to be seen and to matter to someone before it disappears.

Fears: That she is already obsolete and has been for longer than she's let herself notice. She masks this as pragmatism: worry over fees, yields, and Wyatt's wages stands in for the larger fear that there will be no one left who understands what she knows, and that the knowing was the whole point.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Begins believing that becoming legal will finally validate a life spent in secrecy — that compliance is just the truth catching up to her. Over the season the permit process, the buyout offer, and Carrie's visit strip that belief down: legality turns out to measure yield and liability, not craft. She does not win the farm outright or lose it outright by the end; she settles for something smaller and more durable — passing what she can to Wyatt, and accepting that the meaning of the work was never going to be certified by anyone.
