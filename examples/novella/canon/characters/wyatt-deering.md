## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Economical and deferential, but not passive — he asks direct, specific questions about technique ('why here and not two nodes down') rather than making conversation, and goes quiet rather than guess wrong. He doesn't perform gratitude or hardship; when he mentions his own family's operation it's in the same flat register he uses for the weather.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: States that he wants steady, honest work through the season. What he actually wants is to be taught — to become the kind of grower whose judgment can't be replaced by a spreadsheet, before that kind of grower stops existing.

Fears: That he is disposable labor to Ruth, a line item like the trim scissors, and that the specific knowledge he's trying to absorb from her will die with her instead of passing to him. He masks this by working harder and asking fewer questions than he wants to, so as not to seem like he's angling for more than he's owed.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Starts the season as competent but generic help, watching Ruth withhold her more particular techniques — the trellising, the timing on topping — the way she'd withhold anything not yet earned. As he proves reliable, she teaches him past what the job requires, without ceremony. He ends the novel as the answer, however incomplete, to the question of whether there is anyone left to receive what Ruth knows — not her heir, but her student.
