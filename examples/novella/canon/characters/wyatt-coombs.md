## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Old grower's cadence, storytelling, folksy digressions — but increasingly salted with corporate terms he's picked up and doesn't fully trust in his own mouth: SKU, deliverable, compliance audit. Laughs at the end of sentences that aren't funny, to cover the gap.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: Stated: keep working the land he loves in whatever form that takes, even as someone else's employee. Real: to be told he made the right choice selling — to not have sold at all.

Fears: That he traded the only life that ever meant anything to him for a steady paycheck and health insurance, a fear he covers by joking that he was smart, practical, ahead of the curve.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Starts performing contentment with the sale as hard-won wisdom, the smart move nobody else was brave enough to make. Watching himself get managed and phased down by younger corporate staff, and watching Ruth refuse the same offer, cracks the performance open. Ends telling Ruth the truth plainly, once, with nothing left to fix — the clearest mirror of the future she's fighting, offered too late to change his own.
