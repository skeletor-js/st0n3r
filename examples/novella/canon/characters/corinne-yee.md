## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Polished and even, fluent in institutional euphemism — 'transition,' 'legacy partner,' 'streamlined compliance pathway' — delivered at a pace that never rises even when Ruth pushes back. She restates refusals as objections to be revisited rather than as answers, and rarely asks a question she doesn't already have an angle on. She never raises her voice; it isn't a tool she needs.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: States that she wants to secure a fair, mutually beneficial acquisition. What she actually wants is to close the deal — Ruth's operation is a quota line and a career marker, a legacy holdout that would look good converted.

Fears: That she is as replaceable in her company's org chart as she's asking Ruth to become on the land — a fear she manages by over-preparing for every visit and treating each refusal as a personal failure of persuasion rather than a legitimate no.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Begins the season certain the deal is a formality, a matter of finding the right number. Ruth's refusal to be moved by any of her language wears at that certainty without ever converting Corinne to Ruth's side — she does not quit, apologize, or lose the account. By the end she allows herself one unguarded moment, off the record, of seeing what she's actually asking Ruth to give up — and closes her tablet, and does her job anyway.
