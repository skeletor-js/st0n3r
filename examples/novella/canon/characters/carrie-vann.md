## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Brisk and reframing, fluent in the vocabulary of her industry — 'brand story,' 'provenance,' 'legacy positioning' — words she deploys partly to sell her mother on the buyout and partly to keep from saying anything plainer. She interrupts silences before they can become conversations. Under real pressure the marketing language drops out first, and what's left is short and flat, almost like her mother's.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: States that she wants her mother safe, insured, and cashed out before the debt or the DEQ catches up with her. What she actually wants is permission to have left — some proof, from Ruth or from herself, that walking away from the farm wasn't abandonment.

Fears: That she is, underneath the job title, exactly what she left home to stop being: someone who profits off a version of the work with none of the cost. She masks this by treating Ruth's stubbornness as the problem to be managed rather than sitting with her own guilt.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Arrives performing confidence in the buyout and in the life she's built, using competence as a shield against the question of whether she made the right choice. By the end of her visit she stops arguing the numbers and says, plainly, that she never wanted the farm — not as a confession wrung out of her, but as a fact she's finally willing to let stand next to her mother's very different one, without either of them needing to concede.
