## Voice

<!-- How they talk: diction, rhythm, verbal tics, what they never say. -->

Fluent in euphemism and reassurance — 'transition,' 'partnership,' 'right-sizing.' Uses first names too often. Never raises his voice, listens with visible performance of listening, and always circles back to a number by the third sentence.

## Wants / Fears

<!-- Stated want vs. real want. What they're afraid of, and how the fear
     gets masked or rationalized. -->

Wants: Stated: acquire Ruth's license, land, and brand cheaply for the fund's portfolio. Real: to be the closer who can land the hardest, most symbolically loaded deal in the valley, and be seen as such by people above him.

Fears: That he is himself just as replaceable and interchangeable as the growers he's paid to absorb — a fear he buries under confidence, process language, and the certainty that he is the reasonable one in every room.

## Arc

<!-- Where they start, the lie they believe, what breaks it, where they
     end up. -->

Starts treating Ruth as a line item to be closed efficiently and without residue. Watching what the sale did to Wyatt, and watching Ruth refuse to be handled the same way, forces him to see the actual cost of what he sells as inevitability. He does not quit, and he still closes deals — the fund does not care what he sees — but he ends the book having lost the ability to unsee it, and offers Ruth one real concession that costs him something with his own employer.
