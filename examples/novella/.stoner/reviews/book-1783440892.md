## Overall

The prose is controlled and the procedural detail (setback numbers, dates, acreages) checks out consistently across all four chapters, which is real craft discipline — this is a writer who does the arithmetic. The load-bearing problem is that Ruth's interiority resolves through a small set of recurring rhetorical moves — the explicit epiphany-gloss and the 'not X, but Y' antithesis — used often enough in just four chapters that they read as authorial habit rather than character voice, flattening moments that should feel singular. Wyatt, meanwhile, is asserted as significant more than he is dramatized as a person. None of this breaks the manuscript; it's a strong draft with a clear, disciplined voice that needs to trust its own concrete detail more and its summarizing reflexes less.

## Findings (4)

### Chapter 1

- **[major]** (over-explaining / stated theme) L15: The scene with the discarded/kept cutting already dramatizes the point (Wyatt keeps the bad cutting to see if it roots). The narration then states the lesson as an explicit aphorism, closing off the inference the reader was already making. This same move — dramatize, then gloss the meaning in an abstract closing clause — recurs almost verbatim through the book: ch.2's 'not as an injustice done to her personally... but as the shape of a machine built without her in the room,' ch.3's 'she had just answered a question she hadn't known she was still asking,' and ch.4's 'understood, without any particular drama attached to the understanding, that...'. Repeated often enough, it stops reading as Ruth's habit of mind and starts reading as the author's habit of narration. -- `and the difference between those two kinds of people was, in her experience, the whole difference in a grower.`
  - suggestion: Cut the explicit gloss here and let the kept cutting stand on its own — the chapter's own closing image (hands flat on the table, looking at the packet 'the way she might have looked at a frost warning') shows you already know how to land a beat on image alone. Audit the manuscript for sentences that end '...and she understood that...' or '...which was, in her experience, the whole difference in...' and cut at least half of them outright.

### Chapter 2

- **[major]** (syntactic tic (balanced antithesis)) L53: The 'not X, but Y' frame is used as a rhetorical trump card at least five times across only four chapters (the eggs scene in ch.1, this line and 'not because she hadn't understood... but because she had understood it completely' both in ch.2, the pruning-cost passage in ch.3, and 'its own kind of mercy, or its own kind of practice' in ch.4). Each instance is individually well-turned, but stacked this close together the device reads as a verbal tic rather than a considered choice, and it drains weight from the one or two places per chapter where it should really land. -- `not as an injustice done to her personally, which would at least have had a face on it, but as the shape of a machine built without her in the room when its shape was decided, running exactly as it had been built to run.`
  - suggestion: Pick the single most load-bearing 'not X, but Y' per chapter and cut or rewrite the rest into plainer declaratives. Vary the syntax so the antithesis frame feels earned when it appears — try landing an insight as pure image with no framing clause at all, the way you already do at the end of chapter 2 ('and did not stop, and did not turn the radio on').

### Chapter 3

- **[minor]** (underdeveloped secondary character) L45: Wyatt is constructed entirely out of Ruth's inference — his nods, his silences, what she 'noted' and 'filed away.' In four chapters he has no line of dialogue that isn't a direct response to her prompt and no action she didn't predict or cause. If the succession question (is he worth the eleven seasons of unwritten knowledge) is meant to carry real stakes, the reader currently has only Ruth's narration of her own trust, not independent evidence for it. -- `None of it was hers. He'd brought most of it from wherever his family's operation had been, some version of this same knowledge learned on some other hillside from somebody who wasn't her, and what she was doing wasn't teaching him to grow.`
  - suggestion: Give Wyatt one moment — even a small one — that Ruth notices but didn't set up or ask for: a choice, an opinion volunteered unprompted, something that runs slightly against her reading of him. It doesn't need to be dramatic; it needs to originate with him rather than with her observation of him.
- **[minor]** (unglossed reference) L59: Carrie is named exactly once, in a sentence that assumes she carries real emotional weight for Ruth (someone she has deliberately avoided this conversation with). Nothing before this line establishes who Carrie is — daughter, former partner, sibling — so the sentence asks the reader to feel a connection the text hasn't yet supplied. -- `she'd started keeping score of who might still be standing here after she wasn't, and that until this exact moment she had not let herself do that arithmetic out loud, not with Wyatt, not with Carrie, not with anyone.`

If she isn't coming back, cut the name and let the sentence end on 'not with anyone.'
