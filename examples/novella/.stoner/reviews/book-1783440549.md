## Overall

This is a controlled, confident opening with a clear thematic engine — embodied, unwritten knowledge versus bureaucratic measurement — and the Corinne/Pratt/county material earns its antagonism without becoming cartoonish; Ruth's expertise is dramatized in concrete craft (topping nodes, mother-room genetics) rather than merely asserted. The manuscript's main liability is that nearly every scene resolves through the same rhetorical engine — a closing 'not X, but Y' thesis sentence that explains the scene's meaning instead of trusting the preceding image to carry it — which flattens four otherwise distinct chapter endings into one recognizable cadence, and the same flatness bleeds into dialogue, so Corinne, Pratt, and Ruth's narration are hard to tell apart by voice alone. There is also a numeric inconsistency in Chapter 4 (quarter-acre vs. 17,400 sq ft) that needs reconciling given how much of that chapter's authority rests on exact figures. None of this breaks the book, but it is worth a revision pass before moving forward.

### Chapter 1

- **[major]** (over-explaining / thesis-statement endings) L59: The chapter closes by stating its own meaning outright rather than trusting the preceding image (hands flat on the table, the packet unmoved) to carry it. This is the first instance of a pattern that recurs at the close of Chapters 2 and 3 almost verbatim in structure: a long final sentence that explicitly names the emotional or thematic upshot of the scene. -- `not with any decision yet, just with the plain understanding that whatever she did next, she could no longer say she hadn't known.`
  - suggestion: Cut the explanatory clause and end on the physical beat: 'She put both hands flat on the table on either side of the packet, not moving it, and looked at it a while longer.' Let the frost-warning simile do the thematic work implicitly rather than spelling out 'she could no longer say she hadn't known.'

### Chapter 2

### Chapter 3

### Chapter 4

- **[major]** (continuity / arithmetic error) L29: Corinne describes the full-sun plot as 'a quarter-acre' (≈10,890 sq ft), but the site-notes email later in the same chapter lists 'Full-sun cultivation area, approx. 17,400 sq ft' for what is clearly the same plot — a discrepancy of roughly 60%. Because this chapter's authority rests entirely on precise, trustworthy numbers (38 ft, 74 ft, dollar figures), a reader who does the arithmetic on this one will start to distrust the others. -- `This whole quarter-acre is uncured Category Two under the current file,`
  - suggestion: Reconcile the two figures — either have Corinne say 'this whole four-tenths of an acre' to match 17,400 sq ft, or change the email's figure to ~10,890 to match 'quarter-acre.'
- **[major]** (uniform voice) L51: Corinne's dialogue shares the same flat, aphoristic, plainspoken cadence as Ruth's own narrating voice and as Pratt's dialogue in Chapter 2 ('The review's on the acreage as proposed, not as it existed historically'). Covering the attribution tags, it is difficult to tell Corinne, Pratt, and Ruth's interior narration apart by ear — all three deliver short, declarative, faintly wise sentences with a similar rhythm of restraint. -- `I want to be direct with you, Ruth, because I don't think you respond to the other kind.`
  - suggestion: Give Corinne a distinguishable register from Ruth's — more corporate hedge-language, softer euphemism, 'per the current schedule' phrasing that Ruth's plainspokenness can visibly cut against — so the tension between them is audible in the dialogue itself, not just asserted by the narration.
- **[minor]** (deflated suspense) L37: This scene is staged with the beats of a reveal (crouching, sighting the rangefinder, red digits Ruth can see), but the number is not news — Chapter 1 already gave the reader this exact figure ('thirty-eight feet from the old Ostrander line') as Ruth's own 'rough count,' and the seventy-four-foot watercourse figure is likewise pre-disclosed. The scene wants to be about officialization rather than discovery, but its staging borrows the grammar of a discovery scene without delivering new information. -- `The rangefinder read out in red digits Ruth could see from where she was standing. Thirty-eight feet.`
  - suggestion: Either make Ruth's Chapter 1 estimate genuinely approximate ('short of fifty, she didn't want to know by how much') so Chapter 4's precise numbers land as real information, or strip the suspense staging here (the crouching, the sighting) and write the beat explicitly as confirmation-not-revelation, which is what the surrounding narration already argues the scene is about.
- **[minor]** (recycled sentence construction) L59: This is a near-exact structural repeat of a sentence from Chapter 2 — 'which she found, without deciding to find it, more unsettling than if he had' (describing the man with the laptop bag) — applied to a different character sixty-some pages later. The recurrence of this specific 'X's absence of a reaction is more unsettling than the reaction would have been' construction reads as a formula rather than an observation freshly arrived at. -- `Corinne didn't argue the point, which was, in its way, more unsettling than if she had.`
  - suggestion: Cut one of the two instances, or replace this one with something that characterizes Corinne specifically rather than reusing the shape of an earlier, unrelated observation about a stranger in a waiting room.
